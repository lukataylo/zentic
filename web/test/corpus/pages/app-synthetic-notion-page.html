Engineering Handbook

We optimise for the ability to change things later. Most of what follows is a consequence of that: small pull requests because they are easy to revert, boring dependencies because they are easy to replace, and written decisions because a decision nobody wrote down cannot be revisited, only re-argued.

Reviews are for correctness and for shared understanding, in that order. If a change is correct but nobody else understands why it is correct, it will be broken within the quarter by someone acting reasonably. So a review comment asking "why" is never noise, and answering it in the code beats answering it in the thread.

On-call

The on-call rotation is one week, one primary, one secondary, and it starts on Wednesday so that a bad handover does not land on a Friday evening. Pages that fire twice without a runbook get a runbook before the shift ends; pages that fire without being actionable get deleted, because an alert nobody can act on trains everyone to ignore alerts.

Anything that woke someone up gets written up within two working days, blameless, with a timeline and one owner per follow-up. The write-up is not the point either — the follow-ups are, and a write-up with no owned follow-ups is a document we will read again after the same outage.

We do not have a formal severity ladder beyond "is a customer affected right now". Adding one has been proposed twice and both times the discussion consumed more time than the ambiguity ever cost.
